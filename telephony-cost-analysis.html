FirstDigital carrier invoice breakdown paired with Call Tracking revenue, January 2024 through September 2026. The single most actionable finding

A 20% discount that applied to every recurring line item and every LD-usage minute — visible on every invoice from at least January 2024 through November 2025 — disappeared on the December 2025 invoice and has not returned. All other unit prices on the bill are unchanged. The effective rate went up 25% overnight (removing a 20% discount = 1 / 0.80 = 25% price increase). At current volumes that is worth roughly $200,000 / year. This aligns with FirstDigital's contract auto-renewal language on every invoice.

What actually drove the 2026 cost increase

Comparing November 2025 (the last month with the 20% discount) to August 2026 usage (billed on the Sept 2026 invoice) — total current-period charges grew from $88,056 to $162,741, a +$74,684 / month jump.

Rate change — worth ~$22K/mo 30% of delta

FirstDigital's contract auto-renews every 12 months at the rates on the service agreement. A "20% discount" line appeared on every invoice from at least Jan 2024 through Nov 2025 — then vanished without explanation on the Dec 2025 invoice and has not returned. Base per-unit prices did not change; the effective price went up 25% because a 20% discount removed is a 25% rate increase.

Volume growth — worth ~$53K/mo 70% of delta

Domestic InterState minutes went from 2.51M (Nov 2025) to 5.35M (Aug 2026) — more than doubled in nine months. Domestic IntraState grew 60% and Toll-Free InterState 40% over the same window. This is the primary financial driver and needs a mix explanation (client growth, Redd migration traffic, or duration creep).

Number-count trend — is inventory growth driving the cost?

A natural hypothesis: costs are up because we have more phone numbers on the platform (especially vanity numbers). The bills answer that question directly. Every FirstDigital invoice bills five separate number-inventory line items, all at $0.16 per number per month. Below is the count of each, month by month, across all 33 bills.

Verdict: number growth is NOT the cost driver in 2025–2026. Total inventory grew +11.8% from Jan 2024 to Sep 2026, but essentially all of that growth happened during 2024. Since Jan 2025, total number count has been flat to slightly declining (−0.3%). Vanity/premium pools are actually smaller today than they were 2 years ago. The number-count fixed cost is ~$22,394/mo today vs. ~$22,471/mo in Jan 2025 — a $77/month difference. The cost spike is entirely from usage intensity on a stable base of lines.

Every line billed at $0.16/number/month. Total inventory = sum of all five layers. Black line traces the total. Source: 33 FirstDigital invoices, Jan 2024 through Sep 2026.

What actually happened

  1. All of the number growth happened in 2024. The "DID Charge" pool (the orange band) nearly doubled from 11,632 in Jan 2024 to 22,855 in Jan 2025 — a +97% year. This is when Call Tracking's bulk DID inventory was scaling.
  2. Growth stopped in Q1 2025. DID Charge peaked at 25,013 in Mar 2025, then declined back to ~22,000 by mid-year and has held there ever since. Net movement Jan 2025 to Sep 2026: −3.6%.
  3. All other pools are flat. Natl Inbound DID (~72K) hasn't moved. Tollfree Service (~42.5K) has been flat since Mar 2025. Toll Free Number (977) has been exactly the same value for all 33 months.
  4. Vanity/premium counts are DOWN, not up. The two smaller pools most likely to represent vanity/premium lines (DID Number + Toll Free Number) went from 3,366 lines in Jan 2024 to 3,079 today — a loss of 287 lines (−8.5%).

Cost implication

At $0.16 per number per month, the entire number-count inventory drives a fixed monthly cost that has been essentially flat since Jan 2025:

The 20-point margin cliff

Call Tracking gross margin hit a peak of 73.3% in January 2025, held above 65% for most of 2025, then slid steadily through 2026. September 2026 came in at 53.9% — a 19-point compression from the peak. Revenue grew +24% Sep 2026 vs Sep 2025, but cost grew +49% over the same window. Pricing power exists (rev per transaction is up), but not fast enough to offset the carrier cost hike.

Call Tracking monthly gross margin, Jan 2024 – Sep 2026. Green marker = FY 2025 peak; red marker = current low. Reference lines show the FY 2025 average (69.3%) and a 60% healthy-margin floor. Source: Call Tracking revenue (recurring + per-minute + vanity) matched to FirstDigital carrier invoice by month.

The compression drivers, ranked

1. Carrier rate reset (Dec 2025): The 20% discount removal took ~10 points of gross margin off the bill immediately. Comparing Nov 2025 (72.7% GM) to Dec 2025 (67.5%) shows the direct hit.

2. Volume-driven variable cost spike (Apr–Sep 2026): Domestic InterState minutes doubled. Call Tracking revenue is largely fixed per tracked number — minutes are pure cost. Every extra minute of caller talk time drops straight through as margin loss.

3. Transaction count is flat, minutes are not: Transactions grew only +5% Sep '25 → Sep '26 (13,567 → 14,206), but FirstDigital minutes were up +49%. The average tracked call is materially longer than a year ago.

If the trajectory holds, FY 2026 gross margin lands near 60% — down 9 points from FY 2025

Q4 2026 at the September run rate implies FY 2026 gross margin of ~60–61% (versus 69.3% in FY 2025). Restoring the 20% carrier discount alone would move FY 2026 GM back to ~66%. Combined with even a partial volume rationalization (or renegotiation to per-minute pricing more aligned with tracked-number pricing), a return to 68%+ is realistic — but requires acting on both the pricing and the volume conversations before year-end.

Recommended actions

  1. Pull the FirstDigital contract and find the November 2025 renewal event. Every prior invoice showed a 20% discount as a discrete line item. December 2025 removed it silently, and every 2026 invoice has run at 100% of base rates. Determine whether this was an automatic renewal at higher terms, a promotional discount that expired, or a billing error. Contract renewal language on every invoice says the term auto-renews at "the rates shown on your service agreement" — that agreement is the artifact we need. $200K/yr at stake
  2. Investigate the April 2026 volume inflection. Domestic InterState minutes jumped from 2.51M (Nov 2025) to 3.45M (Apr 2026) with no corresponding growth in DID count or line count — meaning existing lines got much busier. Candidates: Redd migration traffic hitting FirstDigital trunks, a new LC client onboarding, longer average handle time, or an outbound-calling product change. Pull LC call volume from Domo (`lc-calls-bpo`) and reconcile. $50K/mo running impact
  3. Get a competitive rate quote before the next auto-renewal. The FirstDigital contract auto-renews 45 days before term end unless action is taken. Get pricing from Twilio Programmable Voice, Bandwidth, and one incumbent SIP alternative on today's minute mix (10.6M/month). The $0.009/min base rate is above market for inbound domestic long-distance at this volume — CPaaS providers typically quote $0.006–$0.0085/min at similar tiers. Term-end lever
  4. Reconcile invoice totals to Austin's accounting table. Invoice totals from FirstDigital PDFs do not exactly match the cost table in the source screenshot (e.g., Aug 2026 invoice $144,895 vs table $158,568). Difference likely stems from accounting-date bookings, prior balances, or LC-specific allocations. Confirm with Finance which figures should be used for the FY 2026 budget forecast. Data hygiene
  5. Tie this to Call Tracking gross margin, not just LC. Call Tracking GM has fallen from 73.3% (Jan 2025) to 53.9% (Sep 2026) because FirstDigital is the primary cost of service. Why this matters as an accounting problem

Right now these costs likely land in an infrastructure or R&D line item rather than an LC cost-of-service line, which means LC gross margin reporting excludes them entirely. Before the FY 2026 close and the FY 2027 budgeting cycle, decide:

  • Which of these belong in LC COGS? A media server dedicated to LC voice is COGS. A shared database that also serves other Entrata systems is not (or only partially).
  • Who owns each line? "LC engineering," "shared infra," or "Entrata platform." Ownership determines which team has the mandate to optimize each.
  • Where does this live going forward? One shared internal dashboard covering carrier + infra + shared services would let anyone answer "what does one call cost us end-to-end?" — a question this analysis can't answer today.
