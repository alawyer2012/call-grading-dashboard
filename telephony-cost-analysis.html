The single most actionable finding

A 20% discount that applied to every recurring line item and every LD-usage minute — visible on every invoice from at least January 2024 through November 2025 — disappeared on the December 2025 invoice and has not returned. All other unit prices on the bill are unchanged. The effective rate went up 25% overnight (removing a 20% discount = 1 / 0.80 = 25% price increase). At current volumes that is worth roughly $200,000 / year. This aligns with FirstDigital's contract auto-renewal language on every invoice.

What actually drove the 2026 cost increase

Comparing November 2025 (the last month with the 20% discount) to August 2026 usage (billed on the Sept 2026 invoice) — total current-period charges grew from $88,056 to $162,741, a +$74,684 / month jump.

Cost bridge, monthly current-period charges (usage + recurring + taxes). Volume growth allocation includes tax scaling; rate change allocation includes tax delta driven by higher pre-discount base.

Rate change — worth ~$22K/mo 30% of delta

FirstDigital's contract auto-renews every 12 months at the rates on the service agreement. A "20% discount" line appeared on every invoice from at least Jan 2024 through Nov 2025 — then vanished without explanation on the Dec 2025 invoice and has not returned. Base per-unit prices did not change; the effective price went up 25% because a 20% discount removed is a 25% rate increase.

Volume growth — worth ~$53K/mo 70% of delta

Domestic InterState minutes went from 2.51M (Nov 2025) to 5.35M (Aug 2026) — more than doubled in nine months. Domestic IntraState grew 60% and Toll-Free InterState 40% over the same window. This is the primary financial driver and needs a mix explanation (client growth, Redd migration traffic, or duration creep).

The discount disappeared between Nov 2025 and Dec 2025

The 20% discount was a stable line item on every prior invoice — same percentage, same treatment of both recurring and usage charges. The December 2025 invoice removed the discount line entirely. Every 2026 invoice matches the same pattern.

Recommended actions

  1. Pull the FirstDigital contract and find the November 2025 renewal event. Every prior invoice showed a 20% discount as a discrete line item. December 2025 removed it silently, and every 2026 invoice has run at 100% of base rates. Determine whether this was an automatic renewal at higher terms, a promotional discount that expired, or a billing error. Contract renewal language on every invoice says the term auto-renews at "the rates shown on your service agreement" — that agreement is the artifact we need. $200K/yr at stake
  2. Investigate the April 2026 volume inflection. Domestic InterState minutes jumped from 2.51M (Nov 2025) to 3.45M (Apr 2026) with no corresponding growth in DID count or line count — meaning existing lines got much busier. Candidates: Redd migration traffic hitting FirstDigital trunks, a new LC client onboarding, longer average handle time, or an outbound-calling product change. Pull LC call volume from Domo (`lc-calls-bpo`) and reconcile. $50K/mo running impact
  3. Get a competitive rate quote before the next auto-renewal. The FirstDigital contract auto-renews 45 days before term end unless action is taken. Get pricing from Twilio Programmable Voice, Bandwidth, and one incumbent SIP alternative on today's minute mix (10.6M/month). The $0.009/min base rate is above market for inbound domestic long-distance at this volume — CPaaS providers typically quote $0.006–$0.0085/min at similar tiers. Term-end lever
  4. Reconcile invoice totals to Austin's accounting table. Invoice totals from FirstDigital PDFs do not exactly match the cost table in the source screenshot (e.g., Aug 2026 invoice $144,895 vs table $158,568). Difference likely stems from accounting-date bookings, prior balances, or LC-specific allocations. Confirm with Finance which figures should be used for the FY 2026 budget forecast. Data hygiene
  5. Tie this to Goal #1 (LC gross margin 20% → 35%). At the current $1.55M+ run rate, telephony is up ~$412K vs 2025. Every $100K of telephony savings is roughly 1 point of gross margin at LC's current revenue. Discount restoration alone ($200K/yr) is worth ~2 GM points without any operational change.

Open questions for finance and networking

Contract

Was the November 2025 discount removal an automatic renewal event, an expired promo, or a billing change? Who signed the current service agreement, and what are its renewal windows?

Traffic mix

What drove Domestic InterState minutes from 2.5M to 5.3M in nine months? Is Leasing AI / Redd routing calls through FirstDigital trunks now? Is average handle time up?

Attribution

The bill has three departments (parent, Lehi, Lindon). Is the full $162K/mo allocated to LC COGS, or split with Entrata corp network? A miscoding could distort LC gross margin reporting.

Why this matters as an accounting problem

Right now these costs likely land in an infrastructure or R&D line item rather than an LC cost-of-service line, which means LC gross margin reporting excludes them entirely. Before the FY 2026 close and the FY 2027 budgeting cycle, decide:

  • Which of these belong in LC COGS? A media server dedicated to LC voice is COGS. A shared database that also serves other Entrata systems is not (or only partially).
  • Who owns each line? "LC engineering," "shared infra," or "Entrata platform." Ownership determines which team has the mandate to optimize each.
  • Where does this live going forward? One shared internal dashboard covering carrier + infra + shared services would let anyone answer "what does one call cost us end-to-end?" — a question this analysis can't answer today.
