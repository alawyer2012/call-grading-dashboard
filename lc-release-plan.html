Voice Agents 3

AI IVR Agent CT ELI+ Voice Agents

Phase 2 of AI IVR: replaces the menu with a conversational AI that greets "How can I help you?", classifies caller intent from natural language, and routes to Leasing / Resident / Maintenance / Other. The AI classifies and routes only — it does not answer questions.

Why this matters

Menu-based IVR (Phase 1) still lets solicitors and non-leads game the system by picking "Leasing" when their intent is something else. Conversational classification is more accurate, deflects non-leads before they reach LC agents, and directly moves the LC gross margin needle (Goal #1: 20% → 35%).

After (intended outcome)

AI picks up: "Thank you for calling [Property]. How can I help you?" — no menu options read. Caller states intent in natural language; AI classifies as Leasing / Resident / Maintenance / Other and routes via existing FreeSWITCH rules. Max 2 conversational turns before routing. Silent callers → voicemail (never an agent). Agent-demand → transfer on second demand, flagged for reporting. Multi-intent priority: Maintenance > Resident > Lead > Other.

Who

Property managers (higher routing accuracy). Prospects, residents, and maintenance callers (no menu to navigate). LC agents (fewer misrouted non-leads in queue). Config: Setup → Property → Communication → Call Handling → IVR Configuration.

LC Other Agent LC Voice Agents

The agent takes the message. It does not resolve it — AI handles Resident (non-work-order) and Other calls the same way LC agents do today, then submits to the property.

Why this matters

LC gross margin is 20% against a 35% 2026 target (Goal #1). Live agents burn queue time on message-taking work — Resident ~21%, Solicitor ~12%, Vendor ~3% of the LC IVR mix (Feb–Apr 2026). That's expensive human time for a job with no lookups, no decisions, and no charges to change.

After (intended outcome)

Voice agent greets with property name, classifies, takes a message. Resident → Resident Call Result (Request Call Back when asked). Solicitor/vendor → Other Call Result + Send Message. Junk (abandoned, spam, wrong number, survey, test) → disposition only. Leads and work orders hand off to their existing flows. 911 follows property protocol. Property staff see the same Dashboard → Leasing Center notes they read today — bit-identical payload.

Who

Residents (still get "we'll have the property call you back," now from AI). Property managers (unchanged read path). LC agents (fewer message-take calls in queue). LC ops (same dispositions, AI does the work). Reuses Super Agent; company flag for rollback.

RU Agent UER Voice Agents

The agent explains this invoice. It does not fix it — live AI on 877-825-3995 (the number printed on the back of the ResidentUtility invoice) instead of 100% voicemail.

Why this matters

Every call to 877-825-3995 hits voicemail today. UBS specialists listen and call back — 2 human touches per contact, 0% live answer. FAQ-class questions (what is VCR? how do I pay?) consume the same listen-and-callback labor as true disputes. Alyssa Barlow (UBS) confirmed VCR is the #1 inbound, and canned templates already exist. Tied to Goal #4 (AI utilization on TES labor), not LC margin.

After (intended outcome)

Utilities Super Agent answers 877 live with disclosure, binds by name or invoice number (no ANI), and explains the latest invoice from canned copy + live fields: VCR, invoice line readback (lease charges called out as lease), billing method one-liner, how to pay (portal only — never takes a card). Anything else — refunds, "why did my bill go up," disputes, off-list — routes to today's UBS voicemail bucket. Company flag can rollback to voicemail-first without reprinting invoices.

Who

Residents (primary — live answer instead of voicemail). Utility Billing Specialists (voicemail bucket becomes real work, not "what is RUBS"). TES ops (flag rollback owner; residual path unchanged). No property staff use — 877 is resident-only. No Entrata UI to build.

QA Insights Dashboard LC LC QA

Reporting dashboard for the LC QA team surfacing performance data, dispute trends, and client-complaint patterns — replacing spreadsheet workflows with real-time visibility. Two permission-based views: Admin (managers / supervisors) and QA Specialist (individual, self-service).

Why this matters

QA leadership runs on manual work today — pulling calls from Client Admin and grading them in Google Sheets to compute accuracy, calculating calls-per-hour from agent status logs, and stitching dispute / complaint trends by hand. Specialists have no self-service view of their own metrics.

After (intended outcome)

Admin view = 4 tabs: Dashboard (Total Calls Graded, Avg Score, Fail Rate, Dispute Rate, trend charts, most-missed / disqualifier / disputed question cards), QA Specialists (per-specialist table: Calls Graded, Avg Score, Fail Rate, Disputes, Consistency, Calls/Hr), Disputes (top reasons + most-disputed questions with drill-down), Client Complaints (by category + flagged calls). Specialist view = personal KPIs + weekly volume + score distribution + own disputes. Filters on both: Specialist, Scorecard Type, Date Range, Client/Property.

Who

QA Managers / Supervisors (full team visibility, replaces spreadsheets). QA Specialists (self-service on own performance). LC Ops (dispute + complaint triage). Internal LC tool — no customer surface.

CT Orchestrator Support CT ELI+ Other Items

Umbrella epic making ELI Orchestrator (Super Agent) a first-class routing destination inside Call Tracking. Anchor story: Orchestrator as voicemail-fallback when the property doesn't answer.

Why this matters

Call Tracking routes today to property, IVR menu, voicemail, or LC — Orchestrator isn't natively selectable. Customers who want AI in their call flow bolt it on outside CT, which blocks the obvious play "if the property doesn't pick up, don't send to voicemail — send to Orchestrator." Deflecting to AI before calls hit the LC queue supports Goal #1 (LC GM 20 → 35%); cleaner intent labels feed Call Analysis (Goal #2); differentiates Call Tracker+ (Goal #3).

After (intended outcome)

Orchestrator appears as a selectable destination in Setup → Property → Communication → Call Handling and in the IVR builder — primary, IVR branch, vanity direct, and voicemail-fallback. Documented CT → Orchestrator handoff (caller ID, DNIS, vanity → property mapping, callback context). Orchestrator-handled calls flow into Call Tracking reporting with disposition and intent labels.

Who

Property managers (new routing option in Call Handling). Callers (may reach AI instead of voicemail). LC agents (fewer property-overflow calls in queue). CSM / Implementation (new setup conversation — "AI backup instead of voicemail"). Sales (CT+ differentiator story, pending SKU-gating decision).

Lead Automation Agent CT Agent

Phase 2 of Auto-Lead (Call Tracker+ add-on): closes the loop on Phase 1 (silent guest-card creation) by notifying the active agent in real time and giving them a path to review, correct, or enrich the auto-created lead inside Entrata.

Why this matters

Phase 1 silently creates an Entrata Guest Card for every lead-classified call — but the handling agent has no way to see, verify, or fix what the AI extracted. Phase 2 puts the auto-created lead in front of the agent while the call is still fresh. Key constraint: requires the agent to be logged into the phone system; if they're not, Phase 1 still captures the lead silently — Phase 2 features simply don't appear.

After (intended outcome)

Toast notification within 5s: "Guest Card created for [Name / Unknown Lead] from [Phone]." Auto-dismisses at 10s. Post-call banner persists with "View Lead in Entrata" / "Edit Details." Edit opens a Split-View Verification Screen — AI-extracted fields (name, phone, email, move-in date, source) on the left, call transcript on the right. Saves write back to the guest card via the Phase 1 API. Analytics events on toast shown/dismissed, banner clicks, and verification saves (with fields_changed).

Who

LC agents (real-time notification + one-click correction flow). Property managers (higher lead accuracy, fewer "Unknown Lead" cards sitting stale). Depends on phone-system login adoption to deliver notifications.

When the LC dashboard detects student_leasing_type = "standard", it renders a Standard Student view optimized for US floor-plan leasing (not by-the-bed).

Conditional emails and stored lead preferences omit Gender/Vacancy fields.

Who

LC agents at Standard student properties (cleaner workflow, correct availability logic). Prospects at those properties (Waitlist option when Sold Out).

LC Settings Sync LC Settings

Phase 2 of the LC → property settings integration. After Phase 1 (DEV-312240 Settings Agent for Qualifications & Policies) ships, this epic goes policy by policy — pulling backend property settings into the LC dashboard as read-only, with a direct link to edit the source.

Why this matters

LC maintains its own copy of policy / qualification descriptions (pet policy, income, occupancy, fees, credit, criminal, smoking, etc.) separate from core Entrata property settings. Two sources of truth → configuration drift. When a customer updates pet policy in property settings, the LC knowledge base doesn't reflect it. Supports Goal #1 (LC gross margin 20 → 35%) by eliminating both the setup-time re-entry and the ongoing drift.

After (intended outcome)

Policy text fields become read-only in the LC dashboard, populated from core property settings. The "Required to review / Not required to review" toggle stays editable per policy. A header link directs users to the specific backend settings page for edits. Rolls out one policy at a time — Pet Policy first, then Income Requirements, Occupancy Limits, Fees, Credit History, Criminal History, Smoking Policy. Empty backend field → LC shows an empty state prompting the user to populate it in property settings.

Who

Property managers (single source of truth — edit in property settings, LC reflects immediately). LC agents (always see current policy info on dashboard). Implementation team (fewer setup-time policy questions; centralization workflow simpler). Support (~80% fewer stale-policy tickets, per success metric).
